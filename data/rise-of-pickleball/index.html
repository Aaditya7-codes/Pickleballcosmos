U.S. Pickleball Participation: 4.2M to 24.3M, 2020–2025

SFIA's participation count increased nearly sixfold in five years. The scale is clear; the useful questions are how fast it happened, who is playing, and whether infrastructure kept pace.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 13, 2026

The reported participation series

SFIA counted 24.3 million U.S. pickleball participants in 2025, up from 4.2 million in 2020. That is a 479% increase over five years. On a compound basis, the two endpoints imply roughly 42.1% annual growth.

The pace slowed in 2025, but the base was much larger

The jump from 2023 to 2024 was 45.8%, according to SFIA. Growth from 19.8 million in 2024 to 24.3 million in 2025 was about 22.7%. A lower percentage growth rate at this stage is not the same thing as a small absolute increase: the 2025 count still added roughly 4.5 million participants year over year.

The player base is broadening

SFIA's 2026 Pickleball Single Sport Report says ages 13–24 had the highest participation rate of any age segment. Women represented 42.9% of participants, compared with 38.6% in 2020.

That matters because the sport is often described through an older demographic stereotype. The current participation data no longer supports treating pickleball primarily as a retirement-community activity.

Participation and infrastructure are different datasets

USA Pickleball's 2025 Annual Growth Report lists 82,613 known courts across 18,258 locations and 104,828 members. Those figures measure different things:

  • 24.3 million is an SFIA participation estimate.
  • 82,613 courts is a count from a court/location database, not a complete census of every playable court.
  • 104,828 members is USA Pickleball membership, not total participation.

Combining them as though they are three measures of the same population would be misleading. Used together, they show a sport whose participant base has expanded faster than any single governing-body membership count can capture.

For the broader national picture, see The State of Pickleball in the U.S. 2026. For the infrastructure side specifically, our U.S. Court Infrastructure Index compares listed courts across all 50 states in both absolute and per-capita terms.

What the five-year change does — and does not — prove

The 479% figure establishes exceptional participation growth. It does not, by itself, tell us retention, spending per player, court utilization, profitability of facilities, or the long-term ceiling for the sport. Those require separate datasets.

For Pickleball Cosmos, that distinction is central to how we cover growth: participation is one measure of the market, not a substitute for the market.

Method note

The figures above are reported by SFIA for U.S. participants age six and older under its participation methodology. The 42.1% compound annual growth rate and 22.7% 2024–2025 growth rate are Pickleball Cosmos calculations from the published endpoints. We label calculations separately from reported observations.
