The State of Pickleball in the U.S. 2026

Pickleball is now a large U.S. participation sport with 24.3 million annual participants, a rapidly expanding core-player base and more than 82,000 known courts. This report separates those measures instead of compressing them into one growth number.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialPublished August 12, 2026
2026 snapshot: SFIA counted 24.3 million U.S. pickleball participants in 2025, including 7.5 million core players who played at least eight times during the year. USA Pickleball reported 82,613 known courts across 18,258 locations and 104,828 members at year-end 2025.

These figures describe different parts of the sport. A participant is not necessarily a USA Pickleball member; a court database is not a census of players; and a location can contain one court or many. They should not be added together or used interchangeably.

Participation: 4.2 million became 24.3 million in five years

SFIA reports that U.S. pickleball participation rose from approximately 4.2 million in 2020 to 24.3 million in 2025, a five-year increase of 479%. The recent reported observations are 8.9 million in 2022, 13.6 million in 2023, 19.8 million in 2024 and 24.3 million in 2025.

Pickleball Cosmos calculates a compound annual growth rate of approximately 42.1% between the 2020 and 2025 endpoints. The 2024-to-2025 increase was approximately 22.7%. Both are Cosmos calculations from SFIA-reported participant counts, not separately reported SFIA metrics.

The deceleration in percentage growth is mathematically unsurprising as the base becomes larger. Adding 4.5 million participants to a 19.8 million base produces a lower percentage rate than adding a similar number to a much smaller base. The more useful question is whether absolute participation, repeat play and infrastructure continue to expand together.

The core-player base is now 7.5 million

SFIA defines core pickleball participants as people who play at least eight times per year. That group increased from 1.4 million in 2020 to 7.5 million in 2025.

Core players represented roughly 30.9% of all 2025 participants, based on Pickleball Cosmos' calculation from SFIA's 7.5 million core and 24.3 million total figures. In 2020, the corresponding ratio was about 33.3%.

The slight difference in those ratios should not be read as evidence that engagement is weakening. The total participant base expanded so quickly that millions of newer or less-frequent players entered the denominator. The absolute core population still grew by more than six million people.

Pickleball's age profile no longer fits the retirement-sport stereotype

SFIA's 2026 Single Sport Report says ages 13–24 now have the highest participation rate of any age segment. Women account for 42.9% of participants, up from 38.6% in 2020.

Those two findings matter more than the familiar claim that pickleball is simply “getting younger.” They show that the participant mix is broadening at the same time as the overall population is growing. That creates a different market for clubs, equipment companies, schools, tournament operators and media than a sport concentrated primarily among older recreational players.

Infrastructure: more than 82,000 known courts

USA Pickleball's 2025 Annual Growth Report cites 82,613 known courts across 18,258 locations in the Pickleheads database. The comparable 2024 report listed 68,458 courts and 15,910 locations.

The court total grew faster than the location total, which is consistent with a market adding capacity at both new and existing venues. It does not, by itself, tell us how many courts were newly constructed versus newly discovered or newly entered into the database.

Important limitation: “known courts” is a database count, not a complete federal census of pickleball infrastructure. It is useful as a tracked series, but it should not be presented as every playable court in the United States.

Membership rose faster than participation in 2025

USA Pickleball reported 104,828 members in 2025, up from 62,260 in its 2024 Annual Growth Report. That is a 68.4% year-over-year increase by Pickleball Cosmos' calculation.

Membership is a measure of the governing body's reach, not the number of people who play the sport. With 24.3 million SFIA participants, USA Pickleball membership represents only a subset of the overall playing population. The two datasets have different definitions and purposes.

The professional game now has a separate equipment-governance layer

One of the clearest signs that pickleball's commercial structure is maturing is that professional equipment regulation no longer maps neatly onto recreational and amateur play.

UPA-A states that its 2026 paddle certification is required for professional competition across PPA and Major League Pickleball events, while amateur competition can continue to use paddles that are currently certified by USA Pickleball even when UPA-A certification is not mandatory.

UPA-A's published 2026 fee schedule lists a $10,000 annual brand fee and $3,000 per-paddle certification fee, with certification valid for 24 months. Its rules apply across PPA and MLP professional events and related pro divisions.

This is not simply an equipment footnote. It means the sport now contains distinct regulatory and commercial layers: recreational participation, USA Pickleball-sanctioned competition and a professional ecosystem operating under UPA-A standards.

Professional economics are becoming material

UPA-A says UPA paid athletes more than $33 million in compensation and winnings in 2025. The figure comes from the professional ecosystem itself and should therefore be treated as a first-party claim rather than an independently audited industry total.

Even with that qualification, the scale is useful. Professional pickleball is no longer defined only by tournament prize purses. Athlete contracts, league economics, sponsorship, broadcast rights, equipment licensing and certification now form part of the market.

Five indicators Pickleball Cosmos will track

A serious annual state-of-the-game series needs stable measures that can be updated without changing definitions every year. Our initial tracking set is:

  1. Total participation: SFIA annual U.S. participant count.
  2. Core participation: SFIA players participating at least eight times per year.
  3. Infrastructure: known courts and locations, with database limitations stated.
  4. Participant mix: age and gender shifts where SFIA releases comparable data.
  5. Institutional scale: governing-body membership plus material changes in professional regulation and economics.

Future editions should add state and metro-level infrastructure, facility economics, equipment-market structure and professional compensation only where sufficiently reproducible source data exists.

Methodology

This report uses public first-party or primary-industry sources available as of August 12, 2026. SFIA is the source for participation and demographic figures. USA Pickleball is the source for its membership and the court/location totals it reports from the Pickleheads database. UPA-A is the source for its professional certification requirements, fees and athlete-compensation claim.

Pickleball Cosmos calculations are explicitly labeled. We do not fill missing years in a reported series with invented observations, and we do not treat membership, court counts and participation as equivalent measures.
