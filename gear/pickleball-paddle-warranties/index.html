Pickleball Paddle Warranties Compared: What 9 Major Brands Actually Cover

A “lifetime warranty” can require registration and still exclude normal surface wear. A one-year warranty can explicitly name delamination and core defects. We read the manufacturers' actual policies rather than ranking brands from marketing badges.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialVerified August 11, 2026
The practical takeaway: warranty length alone is a poor comparison. Check whether registration is required, whether you must be the original purchaser, whether the exact paddle family has a different policy, what structural failures are covered, and whether a replacement inherits or loses coverage.

Do not confuse a warranty with a trial or return window

A return policy answers, “Can I send this back because I changed my mind or it does not suit me?” A warranty answers, “What happens if the product develops a covered defect?” They are different.

That distinction is especially important with paddles because some brands offer a return period that is much shorter than the warranty, while some returns must be unused or may attract a restocking fee. A generous return window does not prove broad structural warranty coverage, and a lifetime defect warranty does not mean you can return a used paddle indefinitely because you no longer like it.

Which brands explicitly mention core or delamination coverage?

Six Zero is unusually explicit on its public policy page: its 12-month structural warranty names core materials, deformation and delamination.

Franklin's C45° and FS Tour policy is also specific. It names structural defects in core or face materials, delamination, cracking or splitting not caused by misuse, manufacturing-related edge-guard separation and certain handle failures.

Other brands may approve a structural failure under broader language covering manufacturing defects or workmanship. But we do not translate generic language into guaranteed coverage for a specific failure mode unless the manufacturer says so.

Where the policies materially differ

Selkirk: strong headline, but check which Selkirk line you are buying

Selkirk Sport's main paddle warranty is a limited lifetime warranty against workmanship and manufacturer defects, activated through registration and limited to the original owner. Normal wear, abuse, negligence, user modification and natural breakdown/fading of graphics or textures over extended use are excluded.

The key shopping detail is that “Selkirk” does not mean one warranty for every paddle carrying a Selkirk-family brand. Selkirk LABS and SLK have separate warranty pages and different durations. That is why our table labels the row Selkirk Sport, not “every Selkirk paddle.”

JOOLA: six months is the default; 12 months requires the extension conditions

JOOLA's standard pickleball paddle warranty is six months from receipt on the original purchase unless otherwise stated. For NFC-enabled paddles, JOOLA says the warranty can be extended to 12 months if the owner registers through the JOOLA Infinity app within 14 days and uploads a receipt that is approved.

That distinction is easy to miss on a retailer page that simply says “12-month warranty.” If the registration and receipt conditions are not completed in time, JOOLA says the standard six-month coverage applies.

Warranty claims are for the original purchaser, and JOOLA excludes unauthorized-retailer purchases. It also states that customers are responsible for shipping back products valued at $100 or more for warranty claims.

CRBN: one year, with retailer-registration and ownership requirements

CRBN gives registered paddles a one-year warranty against manufacturer defects and workmanship. Direct purchases are already registered; products bought from an authorized third-party retailer must be registered within 14 days.

The warranty is non-transferable and limited to the original owner. CRBN excludes normal wear such as edge-guard nicks and grip wear, but specifically says ordinary additions such as edge-guard weights, overgrips and edge-guard tape do not by themselves void the warranty; structural or face modifications can.

Six Zero: one of the clearest structural policies

Six Zero offers a limited 12-month structural warranty and explicitly includes core materials, deformation and delamination. Registration and proof of purchase are required, and the warranty is for the original purchaser.

The exclusions are detailed. They include normal wear, certain edge damage, modifications, some damage associated with clamp-on/third-party weighting systems, and use below the temperature threshold stated in the policy. Replacement paddles are not covered by the warranty.

That specificity is useful because it reduces the need to guess whether a structural term falls inside generic “workmanship” language.

Vatic Pro: straightforward one-year manufacturing-defect warranty

Vatic Pro states that its products carry a one-year warranty from the original purchase date and that issues determined to result from a manufacturing defect are covered. It limits eligibility to the original purchaser.

The policy excludes improper use, normal play usage, negligence, abuse, accidents, faded graphics, worn grip tape and regular wear and tear. Vatic Pro reserves discretion to determine whether a defect exists.

Paddletek: lifetime performance guarantee, but registration matters

Paddletek says it backs paddles with a limited lifetime performance guarantee against manufacturer defects. The paddle must be registered within 14 days of purchase. Coverage applies only to the original owner and is non-transferable.

The public warranty page also limits coverage geographically to the United States and Canada.

Engage: lifetime defect coverage with a 30-day registration rule

Engage publishes a limited lifetime warranty against manufacturer defects and workmanship. Authorized-dealer purchases must be registered within the first 30 days; direct Engage website purchases are automatically registered.

Engage excludes normal wear, abuse, negligence, modifications, graphics fading, damage from striking other objects and unauthorized-dealer purchases. A replacement paddle supplied on an approved warranty claim is not itself covered by the limited lifetime warranty.

Gearbox: one year generally, with named six-month exceptions

Gearbox's current pickleball policy states a one-year limited warranty from the original purchase date for qualifying purchases from Gearbox or an authorized dealer. It explicitly gives G12, G14 and G16 paddles a six-month warranty instead.

The original purchaser must provide proof of purchase and return the defective product for evaluation. Normal wear, texture breakdown, collision damage, repeated ground-strike edge breakdown, permanent modifications and unauthorized/used purchases are among the exclusions.

Franklin: the exact model matters more than the brand name

Franklin is the strongest example of why a brand-level warranty table can mislead. Its general warranty is 90 days against defects in materials or workmanship for the original purchaser.

But Franklin's C45° and FS Tour paddles have their own one-year limited warranty. That premium-paddle policy is unusually detailed: it covers structural defects in core or face materials, delamination/cracking/splitting not caused by misuse, manufacturing-related edge-guard separation, and handle or connection failures caused by construction defects.

It also excludes improper storage including extreme heat or cold. That is a useful reminder that paddle-care behavior can affect a warranty claim.

What “lifetime” actually means here

It does not mean “anything that happens to the paddle forever.” In the policies above, lifetime warranties are still limited warranties against manufacturer defects/workmanship. Normal wear is generally excluded, and registration, original ownership, authorized purchase and proof-of-purchase conditions can still apply.

A paddle face gradually losing texture after heavy play, for example, can be normal wear even when the brand advertises lifetime defect coverage.

Five things to do immediately after buying an expensive paddle

  1. Save the receipt. Proof of purchase is a recurring requirement across these policies.
  2. Register immediately. If a brand has a 14- or 30-day registration window, waiting provides no upside.
  3. Buy from an authorized seller. Several brands expressly exclude unauthorized or resale-channel purchases.
  4. Photograph the paddle and serial/model markings. That makes later documentation easier.
  5. Read the exact model policy. Product-family exceptions exist — Franklin and Selkirk are clear examples.

Which warranty is “best”?

We are not assigning a winner from duration alone.

A lifetime policy can be broad in duration but narrow in what counts as a defect. A shorter policy can be more explicit about core, delamination or edge-guard failure. Claims handling, evidence requirements, shipping costs, replacement coverage and retailer restrictions also affect the real value.

For a buyer worried specifically about structural paddle failure, clarity of structural coverage is at least as important as the headline number of months or years.

Bottom line

Before spending $150–$300 on a paddle, read the warranty with the same attention you give the face material and core thickness.

The patterns are clear: keep your receipt, buy through an authorized channel, register quickly where required, and do not assume “lifetime” covers normal wear. Most importantly, check the exact paddle family rather than relying on a brand-wide claim copied onto a retailer page.
