# Pickleball Cosmos Gear Lab — Provisional Methodology v0.1

Status: internal operating framework. Not yet a claim that Pickleball Cosmos has physically tested any paddle.

## Purpose

Build a repeatable, auditable testing system that separates manufacturer claims, governing-body approval data, independent third-party measurements and Pickleball Cosmos measurements.

The Gear Lab should optimize for four things: repeatability, traceability, consumer relevance and intellectual honesty.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. USA Pickleball / governing-body approval records and equipment standards for legality and compliance.
2. Manufacturer-published specifications for first-party product claims.
3. Peer-reviewed sports-engineering literature for transferable measurement mechanics.
4. Independent specialist measurements, always attributed with method/source where available.
5. Pickleball Cosmos measurements, only after physical measurement under the published Gear Lab protocol.
6. Subjective play impressions, clearly separated from instrumented measurements.

No third-party or manufacturer number may be relabeled as a Cosmos measurement.

## Paddle identity and sample control

Each physical paddle receives a unique sample ID. Record brand, exact model, thickness, shape/variant, color if variant-specific, serial/lot identifier when present, purchase source, acquisition type (purchased, loaned, manufacturer sample), acquisition date, retail price paid and approval-list record.

Test the paddle in stock retail configuration before any overgrip, edge tape or weighting is added. Photograph both faces, edge, handle and model markings before testing. Record any visible manufacturing variation or damage.

Where practical, measure more than one retail sample of high-impact models. A one-sample result must be identified as such and must not be presented as proof of model-wide manufacturing consistency.

## Environmental and preparation controls

Paddles should acclimate indoors before measurement. Record room temperature and relative humidity for instrumented sessions once the Gear Lab begins operating. Use the same preparation state for comparable measurements: clean/dry face, stock grip, no protective cover, no aftermarket tape or weights.

If a paddle is tested after use, record cumulative play time and distinguish NEW, USED and DURABILITY-CHECK measurements.

## Core instrumented measurements — Phase 1

### 1. Static mass

Report grams as the primary lab measurement and ounces as a converted consumer unit.

Target equipment: calibrated digital scale with at least 0.1 g readability; 0.01 g is preferred if stable across the paddle's mass range.

Protocol: zero the scale, measure the complete stock paddle three times, removing/replacing it between readings. Store all readings and publish the mean. If repeat readings do not agree within the lab's validated repeatability threshold, investigate before publishing.

### 2. Dimensions

Record overall length, maximum width, core/paddle thickness, handle length and grip circumference. Use calibrated calipers/rule appropriate to the dimension. Record raw values in millimeters and display inches where useful.

Do not infer official compliance from our dimensional measurement alone; USA Pickleball's approval record controls approval status.

### 3. Balance point / center of mass

Report distance from the butt end of the handle to the balance point in centimeters.

Protocol: use a repeatable balance fixture or knife-edge/roller method with marked reference. Repeat at least three placements. Store raw readings and mean.

Balance point is descriptive; do not invent universal head-light/head-heavy consumer categories unless Cosmos later defines and publishes a dataset-derived classification.

### 4. Swing weight / transverse moment of inertia

Swing weight must always be tied to a defined reference axis. Numbers from different axes or methods are not automatically comparable.

Preferred approach: validated pendulum or commercial inertia device with a fixed paddle mount and explicit pivot distance from the handle butt. Store pivot distance, oscillation periods/raw instrument readings, paddle mass and balance point.

Peer-reviewed racket/padel literature supports physical-pendulum methods for estimating moment of inertia and demonstrates the importance of a fixed axis. The lab must validate its own apparatus using calibration objects and repeatability checks before any Cosmos swing-weight numbers are published.

Publish units as kg·cm² when using the conventional racket-sport scale, with method and reference axis disclosed.

Minimum operating rule: three independent measurement runs after remounting the paddle. The acceptable spread will be fixed only after apparatus validation; do not predeclare a precision the apparatus has not demonstrated.

### 5. Twist weight / polar moment of inertia

Twist weight describes resistance to rotation about the paddle's long axis and requires a different measurement geometry from swing weight.

Preferred approach: validated trifilar/torsional pendulum or equivalent inertia measurement device. Centering of the paddle's mass relative to the rotational axis is a known source of error and must be controlled.

Publish kg·cm² with apparatus/method disclosure. Require repeated remount-and-measure trials and a calibration/validation record before publishing Cosmos twist-weight values.

## Phase 2 measurements — only after validation

### Spin

Do not publish a single 'spin score' until a ball-launch, paddle-motion and imaging/radar protocol is repeatable. Candidate outputs: post-impact ball RPM, launch speed and launch angle under controlled input. Human-player spin tests may be used as supplemental evidence, not as the sole instrumented metric.

### Power / pop

Avoid vague labels. Candidate objective protocols should distinguish rebound response at low-input conditions from high-speed impact response. Any test must control incoming ball speed, impact location, paddle constraint/velocity and ball model/condition.

### Sweet-spot / stability map

Potential future test: controlled impacts across a face grid, recording rebound/deflection and torsional response. This could create a genuinely proprietary forgiveness map rather than relying on a single twist-weight number.

### Durability

Record baseline measurements, controlled/real-world usage, surface wear, mass change, sound/feel changes and repeated performance measurements. Do not call core crushing, delamination or material failure without physical evidence supporting the diagnosis.

## Subjective play testing

Instrumented metrics and play impressions are separate evidence types.

When hands-on play testing begins, record tester identity/level, dominant hand, prior paddle, court/ball conditions and test duration. Use a fixed scorecard for maneuverability, stability, touch, dwell/feel, pop, power, spin confidence and comfort, but never imply subjective scores are laboratory measurements.

Where possible, blind or partially blind comparisons should be used for close competitors.

## Measurement publication rules

Every published Cosmos metric should carry:
- sample ID
- sample count
- measurement date
- method version
- stock/customized state
- units
- mean value
- repeatability/uncertainty field when validated

If the methodology changes materially, new measurements must carry the new method version. Do not silently mix incompatible datasets.

## Calibration and quality assurance

Before launch, the lab must create a calibration log. Validate mass and length instruments against traceable reference objects where practical. Validate inertia apparatus using rigid objects with calculable or independently certified moments of inertia and repeated remounting tests.

Published precision must reflect demonstrated repeatability, not instrument display resolution.

## Commercial independence

Purchased, borrowed and manufacturer-supplied paddles follow the same test protocol. Acquisition source is disclosed. Manufacturers may identify factual errors before publication when appropriate but receive no veto over conclusions, rankings or measurements.

Affiliate economics never change a score or recommendation.

## Current public-claim rule

Until the physical lab is operating and this protocol is validated, Pickleball Cosmos may publish official approval data, manufacturer specs and attributed independent measurements, but must not state that a paddle was 'tested by Pickleball Cosmos.'

## Research basis for the measurement framework

- USA Pickleball Official Rulebook / Equipment Standards and Approved Paddle List.
- Cross & Bower (2006), Journal of Sports Sciences: swing-weight effects and distinction between mass and moment of inertia.
- Towler et al. (2023), Scientific Reports: racket moment of inertia, simple-pendulum measurement and racket-head speed.
- Allen et al./racket inertia literature on eccentric impacts and principal moments of inertia.
- Identifying the Inertial Properties of a Padel Racket (2022): physical and trifilar pendulum designs, calibration and repeatability for multiple inertia axes.

This document is an operating specification, not a marketing page. Revise only when measurement evidence or apparatus validation justifies the change.
