Can You Touch the Net in Pickleball?

No — not while the ball is live. If you, your paddle, your clothing or anything you are wearing or carrying touches the net system while the ball is live, it is a fault. The ball itself is different: a ball can touch the net and remain in play if the resulting shot is otherwise legal.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialChecked August 14, 2026
The ruling: player or equipment touches the net system while the ball is live = fault. Ball touches the net = not automatically a fault. Timing and what made contact matter.

What the 2026 rule actually protects

The rule is not simply "don't touch the net." The controlling condition is that the ball is live. USA Pickleball's current fault framework treats contact with the net system by a player, the player's paddle, clothing or anything the player is wearing or carrying as a fault during that period.

That makes the timing important. A player who brushes the net trying to finish a volley before the rally has ended commits a fault. If the ball has already become dead before the contact occurs, the live-ball net-contact rule no longer decides the rally.

Your paddle counts

A common argument is that only the player's body is prohibited from touching the net. That is incorrect. If the paddle touches the net system while the ball is live, the player has committed a fault.

The same logic applies to clothing and items the player is wearing or carrying. A loose shirt brushing the net during a live rally is not rescued by the fact that the player's skin never touched it.

Crossing the net plane is not the same as touching the net

This is where two different rules are often collapsed into one. A legal follow-through can carry the paddle or part of the player across the imaginary plane of the net after the ball has been struck, provided the player does not violate the separate restrictions on the net system, the opponent's court or the opponent.

There is also a narrow backspin/wind situation in which a ball bounces on your side and returns to the opponent's side without being hit. The rules permit the receiving player to reach across the plane to play that ball once it has crossed back. That exception does not give permission to touch the net system.

For the full plane-of-net ruling, see Can Your Paddle Cross the Net in Pickleball?

The ball may touch the net

The prohibition is on the player-side contact described above, not ordinary ball contact with the net. A rally ball that clips the top of the net and lands legally on the opponent's side remains playable. A serve may also touch the net and continue if it satisfies the current service-placement rules.

That distinction is why "the net was touched" is not enough information to make the call. Ask two questions: what touched it, and was the ball still live?

Net posts are a separate case

USA Pickleball treats the net posts separately from the ordinary net contact a ball can survive. A ball contacting a net post becomes dead. Under the 2026 framework, the result can turn on what happened immediately before that contact.

If a shot hits the net post before making the required legal bounce, the hitter cannot rely on ordinary net-cord rules. If the ball has already bounced legally in the receiving player's court and that player fails to return it before it contacts a permanent object, the receiving player loses the rally under the current permanent-object rule.

This distinction matters in rare backspin or wind-aided rallies. See the permanent-object ruling for the before-bounce/after-bounce logic.

Why this rule gets miscalled

Three different ideas are commonly mixed together: the player touching the net, the ball touching the net, and a player crossing the net plane. They are not interchangeable. A ball can legally clip the net. A player's legal follow-through can cross the plane after contact. But the player cannot use either fact to excuse contact with the net system while the ball remains live.

Bottom line

If the ball is live, keep yourself, your paddle and anything you are wearing or carrying off the net system. If the ball merely clips the net, keep playing unless another rule ends the rally.
