Pickleball Carry Rule: Is a Scoop a Fault?

Yes—if the ball is caught or carried on the paddle. But do not confuse that with a legal double hit: the 2026 rulebook allows more than one contact by one player when it occurs in one continuous, single-direction stroke.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialChecked August 15, 2026
The ruling: Rule 12.C prohibits catching or carrying the ball. Rule 10.D can permit multiple contacts in one qualifying stroke. They are separate questions, and the rulebook does not set a numerical contact-time threshold for a carry.

What the 2026 rule actually says

Rule 12.C is brief: a player must not catch or carry a ball on the paddle during a rally. Rule 12.C.1 makes that action a fault. The rule does not define a carry by a number of milliseconds, a particular paddle angle or the height of the ball.

That missing threshold matters. “It looked like a scoop” may be useful court shorthand, but it is not an additional rulebook test. A close call has to be resolved under the actual prohibition: was the ball caught or carried?

Why a double hit can still be legal

The double-hit rule sits in Rule 10.D, not Rule 12.C. It allows a ball to be hit more than once by one player when the stroke is continuous and in a single direction. The rule regulates the player’s stroke; it does not grant a second swing or an opportunity to control the ball on the paddle.

A quick face-and-edge contact in one uninterrupted defensive motion can qualify as a legal multiple contact. A catch-and-sling action is governed by the carry rule instead. For the full multiple-contact test, see Are Double Hits Legal in Pickleball?

Who makes a disputed carry call?

In player-called play, players are expected to call faults on themselves or their partner when they commit or detect them. An opponent may enforce only two types of opponent faults: a non-volley-zone fault and a service foot fault. A suspected carry is neither.

That means an opponent may point out a suspected carry after the rally, but the player alleged to have carried the ball makes the final fault decision. If the player does not call it, the rally result stands. In an officiated match, the referee identifies and calls rule violations.

Common situations, resolved

The ball stays on the paddle during a soft dink

If the player caught or carried it, the shot is a fault. “Soft” is not itself the violation; carrying is.

A player digs a low ball up with an open paddle face

Legal unless the ball was caught or carried. The rulebook does not ban an open paddle face or a low defensive contact.

The ball rattles off paddle face and edge

That can be a legal double hit if one player made one continuous, single-direction stroke. It is not automatically a carry merely because there was more than one contact.

Bottom line

Do not call a carry based on a paddle angle, a slow shot or the fact that the ball had multiple contacts. The current rule prohibits catching or carrying; it separately permits qualifying multiple contacts in one stroke. In player-called play, a carry ultimately belongs to the player who made the shot to call.
