Pickleball Permanent Object Rule: What Happens if the Ball Hits a Fence, Wall or Ceiling?

The timing matters. If a live ball hits a permanent object before bouncing in the opponent's court, the hitter loses the rally. If it first bounces legally in the opponent's court and then hits a permanent object, the ball is dead and the player who hit it wins the rally.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialChecked August 14, 2026
The ruling: permanent object before the legal bounce = fault on the hitter. Legal bounce first, permanent object second = dead ball; hitter wins the rally.

The decision table

SequenceRuling
Ball hits a fence, wall, ceiling or other permanent object before bouncing in the opponent's courtFault on the player/team that hit the ball.
Ball first bounces in the opponent's court, then hits a permanent objectDead ball; the player/team that hit the ball wins the rally.
Stray ball from another court enters the playing areaPotential hinder, not a permanent-object ruling.

Why players confuse this with a hinder

A permanent object is fixed to the playing environment. A hinder is a transient outside occurrence that adversely affects play. A fence does not become a hinder because it interferes with a shot; a stray ball rolling onto the court can be one.

Example: lob hits the back fence

If your lob clears the opponent but strikes the back fence before touching their court, the shot is out of play and you lose the rally. If the lob lands in bounds first and then reaches the fence, the rally is already decided in your favor when the permanent object is contacted.

What counts as a permanent object?

The governing framework treats fixed parts of the playing environment separately from transient interference. Common examples include fences, walls and other fixed structures around or above the court. Tournament layouts can create unusual cases, so the current rulebook and officiating guidance control.

Related rulings matter

Do not apply this rule to every unusual contact. Net-system contacts, player contacts, hinders and distractions have their own rules and can produce different outcomes.

Quick rule

Before bounce: hitter loses.
After legal bounce: hitter wins.
