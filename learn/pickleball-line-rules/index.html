Pickleball Line Rules: What Is In or Out in 2026?

During a rally, a ball that touches a court line is in. The important exception is the serve: the ball must clear the non-volley zone and its line, so a serve landing on the kitchen line is a fault.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 10, 2026
The short version: during ordinary rally play, any ball touching any court line is in. On the serve, the baseline, sideline and centerline of the correct service court are in, but the non-volley-zone line is not: a served ball touching the kitchen line is short and therefore a fault.

1. A ball touching a line is normally in

For ordinary rally play, USA Pickleball treats a ball as in when it lands in the court or touches a court line. The ball does not need to land mostly inside the line. If the ball actually touches the line, it is in.

The useful visual test is not where the center of the ball appears to be. The question is whether the ball contacted the line. A ball can look mostly outside and still be in if part of its contact with the playing surface catches the line.

2. The kitchen line is different on the serve

The non-volley-zone line — commonly called the kitchen line — is the important exception. A legal serve must clear the non-volley zone and its lines before landing in the correct diagonal service court.

That means a serve landing on the kitchen line is short and is a fault. By contrast, a serve landing on the correct service court's centerline, sideline or baseline is in.

Memory rule: on a serve, the kitchen line is out; the other boundary lines of the correct service court are in.

3. The kitchen line is part of the kitchen

There is a second reason the non-volley-zone line matters. For non-volley-zone rules, the boundary line is part of the zone itself. If a player's foot is touching the kitchen line while volleying, that player is touching the non-volley zone and commits a fault.

This does not conflict with the general rule that a ball touching a line is in. One rule concerns where the ball landed; the other concerns whether a player is touching the non-volley zone during a volley.

4. Who makes the line call?

USA Pickleball's 2026 rulebook places line calls in Section 8. In normal player-called play, players are responsible for line calls on their end of the court, subject to the separate procedures used when referees or line judges are assigned in tournament play.

The basic sportsmanship principle is deliberately demanding: close calls are resolved in favor of the opponent. If you cannot clearly call a ball out, it is treated as in. The rules do not give a player a replay simply because the player did not see the ball clearly.

5. An out call requires certainty

An “out” call is not supposed to be a guess. Player line-call standards intentionally give the opponent the benefit of uncertainty. If the landing cannot be called out with the required certainty, the ball is in.

This is one of the most useful differences between pickleball's written line-call code and casual arguments about what a player “thinks” happened. Uncertainty is not a 50/50 decision. It favors the opponent.

6. What happens when doubles partners disagree?

The 2026 rules sharpened the terminology here. If one partner calls a ball out and the other calls it in, conflict exists and the team's call is in.

That wording matters. USA Pickleball changed the older “doubt” language because some players treated a partner saying “I didn't see it” as though it automatically created a conflicting call. The 2026 clarification focuses on actual partner disagreement.

7. Can you overrule your partner?

Yes, to your own team's disadvantage. If your partner calls a ball out but you clearly saw it in, the proper call is in. The rules' line-calling framework is designed around accuracy and giving the opponent the benefit of close or conflicting calls.

The same principle is why a player may acknowledge that a ball the player hit was out on the opponent's side when doing so disadvantages the player's own team.

8. Can spectators help with a line call?

No. The 2026 rules expressly state that spectators must not be consulted on calls. A crowd member, coach, friend or parent may have had the perfect angle, but that does not make the person part of the officiating process.

This protects both the integrity and the pace of play. A disputed landing is resolved through the players and, where applicable, the assigned officials — not by polling the sideline.

9. What changes in an officiated tournament?

The core “in” and “out” geometry does not change, but responsibility for making or reviewing calls can. The 2026 rulebook separates general line-call rules in Section 8 from tournament line-call procedures in Section 19. Matches may be played without line judges or with them, and the rulebook provides procedures for appeals, referee calls and player overrides to their own disadvantage.

That distinction is important because a recreational game with four players calling their own lines is not procedurally identical to a sanctioned match with a referee and line judges.

Quick answers

Is the line in or out in pickleball?

Normally in. A ball touching a court line during a rally is in.

Is the kitchen line in on a serve?

No. A serve must clear the non-volley zone and its line. A serve landing on the kitchen line is a fault.

Is the centerline in on a pickleball serve?

Yes, when it is the centerline bordering the correct diagonal service court.

Is the baseline in?

Yes. A ball touching the baseline is in; the same is true for a serve landing on the baseline of the correct service court.

What if I cannot tell whether the ball was out?

It is in. Player line-call standards give the opponent the benefit of uncertainty.

What if partners make different calls?

If one calls out and the other calls in, the team call is in.
