Pickleball Hinder Rule: When Is a Rally Replay?

A hinder is a temporary outside occurrence that adversely affects play. A valid hinder stops the rally and results in a replay; permanent objects, distractions and player-created equipment problems are handled differently.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 13, 2026
The ruling: a stray ball entering from another court is the classic hinder. Stop play promptly. In officiated play, the referee decides whether a player-called hinder is valid. A valid hinder is replayed.

What counts as a hinder?

USA Pickleball’s governing definition centers on a transient occurrence not caused by a player. Permanent objects are excluded. Examples in governing material include stray balls, foreign material and people from another court when the occurrence adversely affects play.

SituationRuling
Ball rolls in from another courtPotential hinder; stop play promptly.
Person from another court enters the playing areaPotential hinder.
Fixed fence or wall affects your playNot a hinder simply because it was in the way.
Opponent commits a prohibited distractionHandled as a distraction fault, not a hinder replay.
Your carried spare ball falls to the playing surface while the ball is liveFault under the 2026 extra-ball rule.

Why permanent objects are different

The word transient matters. A fence, wall or other fixed feature does not become a hinder merely because it affects a shot. Permanent-object outcomes are covered by their own rules.

A distraction is not a hinder

A hinder comes from an outside occurrence. A prohibited distraction comes from player behavior. The 2026 rulebook separates those concepts, which matters because the outcome can be different: a valid hinder leads to a replay, while a distraction can be a fault.

Dropping a spare ball is a fault

USA Pickleball strengthened this point for 2026. In non-officiated tournament play, an extra ball carried by a player must remain out of the opponent’s view and stay in the player’s possession. If it becomes visible or falls to the playing surface while the ball is live, the player commits a fault.

What about a net malfunction?

The 2026 rulebook has a specific replay rule for net-system malfunctions. That is different from an ordinary ball contact with a permanent part of the net support system.

Do not use rejected proposal language as a current rule

A 2026 proposal sought to add a more detailed test for deciding whether a transient occurrence actually affected a player’s opportunity to make a play. USA Pickleball disapproved it. The practical lesson is important: current rulebook language controls, not proposed wording copied from the rule-change database.

Rulebook map

The 2026 rulebook reorganization groups general dead balls, faults and hinders in Section 9, with related net and tournament procedures in their own sections. Older articles may use pre-2026 rule numbers.
