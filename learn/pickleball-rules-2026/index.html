Pickleball Rules 2026: The Essentials

A practical map of the rules that decide most points, grounded in the 2026 USA Pickleball Official Rulebook.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 10, 2026

The USA Pickleball Official Rulebook is the definitive source for sanctioned play. This page is the starting point: it explains the structure of the game, then links to deeper guides where the details matter.

The serve

Pickleball allows both a volley serve and a drop serve. The volley serve has three contact requirements: a clear upward paddle arc, a clearly compliant paddle-head position relative to the wrist joint, and ball contact clearly no higher than the server's waist. The drop serve is not subject to those three volley-serve motion restrictions. Both serve types remain subject to positioning, release and placement rules.

The two-bounce rule

The receiver must let the serve bounce. The serving team must then let the return bounce. After one bounce has occurred on each side, either team may volley the ball when otherwise legal.

The important distinction is that this means two required bounces in total, not two bounces on each side.

The non-volley zone

The kitchen is not forbidden territory. Players may enter it and play balls that have bounced. The restriction is on volleying: a player cannot volley while touching the non-volley zone or its line, and momentum from a volley cannot carry the player into it.

Faults

A fault is a rule violation that ends the rally. Common examples include illegal serves, balls hit out or into the net, two-bounce violations, kitchen volleys, double bounces, touching the net while the ball is live and certain player-contact violations. The 2026 rulebook reorganized faults so they generally sit with the rules they enforce rather than in one stand-alone fault section.

Read the full faults guide →

Crossing the plane of the net

A paddle is not automatically illegal merely because it finishes over the net. In normal play, the ball must cross the plane to your side before you strike it; after legal contact, your paddle or body may cross the plane during the follow-through, provided you do not commit a separate net, opponent or court-contact violation. A rare exception applies when a ball bounces on your side and then spins or blows back across the net.

Line calls

During ordinary rally play, a ball touching a court line is in. The serve has one important exception: it must clear the non-volley zone and its line, so a serve landing on the kitchen line is a fault. The 2026 rulebook places general line-call responsibility, certainty, partner disagreement and spectator rules in Section 8.

Read the full line rules guide →

Scoring

Traditional side-out scoring remains fundamental to pickleball: only the serving side scores. In doubles, the score call identifies the serving team's score, the receiving team's score and which server is serving. The special opening service sequence explains why traditional doubles games begin at 0–0–2.

Read the scoring guide →

The court

A standard pickleball court measures 20 by 44 feet. The non-volley zone extends 7 feet from the net on each side. Court lines matter differently depending on the situation: for example, the non-volley-zone line is part of the kitchen, and a serve landing on it is short.
