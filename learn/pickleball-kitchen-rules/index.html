The Pickleball Kitchen Rule, Explained Properly

What the non-volley zone actually prohibits, when you can enter it, and why momentum matters.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 10, 2026

The “kitchen” is the nickname for pickleball’s non-volley zone: the 7-foot area on both sides of the net.

The rule in one line

You cannot volley while touching the kitchen or its boundary line.

Can you step into the kitchen?

Yes. The kitchen is not forbidden territory. You may enter it to play a ball after it bounces, and you may remain there. The restriction is specifically about volleying.

Why momentum matters

If you volley from outside the kitchen and your momentum then carries you into it, the volley is still illegal. The fault is tied to the volley action and its resulting momentum, not merely the position of your feet at ball contact.

Memory rule: kitchen + volley = problem. Kitchen + bounced ball = generally legal.
