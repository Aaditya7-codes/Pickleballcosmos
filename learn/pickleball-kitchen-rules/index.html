Pickleball Kitchen Rules 2026: The Non-Volley Zone Explained

The kitchen is not a place you are forbidden to enter. It is a non-volley zone. That distinction explains almost every kitchen ruling.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 15, 2026
The rule in one sentence: you may stand in, walk through and play a bounced ball from the kitchen; you may not volley while you or anything in contact with you touches the non-volley zone, and momentum from a volley cannot carry you into it.

What is the kitchen?

The official term is the non-volley zone, or NVZ. It extends 7 feet from the net on each side of a standard pickleball court and runs the full 20-foot width of the court. The boundary line belongs to the non-volley zone.

Its purpose is straightforward: players cannot stand next to the net and smash balls out of the air from inside the zone. The rule regulates volleys, not ordinary movement into the area.

1. Yes, you can step into the kitchen

This is the most common beginner misconception. There is no general rule requiring players to stay out of the kitchen.

You may enter before the ball bounces. You may stand there while the rally continues. You may play a dink or any other shot after the ball has bounced—even if that bounce occurred elsewhere on your side. You may also walk through the zone when you are not in the act of volleying.

The restriction begins when you play a volley: a ball struck out of the air before it bounces.

2. The kitchen line counts as part of the kitchen

If any part of your foot touches the non-volley-zone line while you volley, the result is a fault. You do not need to step deep into the painted area; the line itself is enough.

This creates a second line-rule distinction worth remembering. During an ordinary rally, a ball that lands on the kitchen line is in. But for player positioning, that same line is part of the NVZ. And on the serve, a ball touching the NVZ line is short and therefore a fault.

3. Momentum is part of the volley

The kitchen rule does not end at the instant the paddle contacts the ball. If you volley legally from outside the NVZ but the motion produced by that volley carries you onto the line or into the zone, it is still a fault.

That remains true even if the volley was a winner and the ball became dead before you eventually touched the kitchen. Rule 9.B.1 expressly preserves this exception to the usual requirement that faults occur only while the ball is live.

Do not use “the point was already over” as the test. The question is whether the contact with the kitchen was still the result of your volley momentum.

4. Your partner can become part of a kitchen fault

Simply having a partner stand in the kitchen is not a fault. The problem arises when the volleying player, or something connected to that player's volley action, contacts the partner while the partner is touching the NVZ.

Example: you volley from outside the line, lose balance and grab your partner, whose foot is in the kitchen. That contact can make the volley a fault because your momentum caused you to contact something — your partner — that was touching the NVZ.

By contrast, if your partner is standing in the kitchen and you legally volley from outside without contacting them, their location alone does not make your shot illegal.

5. You must re-establish completely outside before volleying again

If you have been touching the kitchen, you cannot simply lift one foot, jump backward and volley while airborne. Before volleying again, both feet must make contact with the playing surface completely outside the non-volley zone.

This is why jumping from inside the kitchen and hitting a ball before landing outside does not erase your previous NVZ position. Re-establishment requires actual contact outside the zone.

6. You can reach over the kitchen line

The non-volley zone is a court area, not an invisible vertical wall. If your feet are legally outside, you may lean or reach over the line and volley a ball in the air, provided neither you nor anything in contact with you touches the NVZ during the volley action.

This is why players routinely take balls out of the air while their paddle is physically over the kitchen. The paddle's position above the painted area is not itself the violation.

Do not confuse this with crossing the plane of the net, which is governed by a different set of rules.

7. What if something you are wearing or carrying touches the kitchen?

The NVZ rule is not limited to shoes. USA Pickleball applies it to the volleying player and anything that has contact with the player during the act of volleying. A paddle, clothing or other connected item contacting the NVZ can therefore produce a fault.

The practical principle is broader than “keep your feet out”: keep your entire volley action clear of the zone.

8. The serve has a separate kitchen-line rule

A legal serve must clear the opponent's non-volley zone and land in the correct diagonal service court. A serve that lands on the kitchen line is short and a fault.

That does not mean the kitchen line is generally “out.” Once the rally is underway, a ball landing on that line is in like other court lines. The special exclusion applies to service placement.

What changed in the 2026 rulebook?

The underlying kitchen concept remains familiar, but the 2026 Official Rulebook was reorganized. Non-volley-zone infractions now sit together in Section 11, rather than being scattered through older rulebook structures.

The current definition supplies the controlling endpoint: volley momentum ends when the player regains balance and control of their motion or stops moving toward the non-volley zone. There is no fixed time or step count. See the focused momentum ruling for the complete test.

Quick answers

Can you stand in the kitchen in pickleball?

Yes. You may legally be in the kitchen whenever you are not violating the volley rules.

Can you hit the ball while standing in the kitchen?

Yes, if the ball has bounced before you hit it. You cannot volley while touching the NVZ.

Can you step in the kitchen before the ball bounces?

Yes. There is no rule requiring you to wait outside for the bounce. The restriction is on volleying, not entering.

Can your paddle be over the kitchen?

Yes. Reaching over the line in the air is not itself a fault. Contact with the NVZ during the volley action is what matters.

Is the kitchen line in or out?

For player positioning, it is part of the kitchen. A rally ball landing on it is in. A serve landing on it is short and a fault.

Can momentum cause a fault after the rally ends?

Yes. If momentum from your volley carries you into the NVZ, the fault can occur even after the ball has become dead.

Do I need both feet outside before I can volley after leaving the kitchen?

Yes. Both feet must make contact with the playing surface completely outside the NVZ before you volley.
