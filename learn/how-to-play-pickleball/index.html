How to Play Pickleball in 2026: A Beginner’s Guide

Pickleball is easiest to understand as a sequence: serve diagonally, let the first two shots bounce, then play the rally — while respecting the non-volley zone and the scoring system.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialUpdated August 10, 2026
The game in 30 seconds: pickleball is played as singles or doubles on a 20 × 44-foot court. The serve goes diagonally into the correct service court. The receiver must let the serve bounce; the serving team must let the return bounce. After that, either side may volley when otherwise legal. Traditional side-out scoring awards points only to the serving side.

1. Know the court

A regulation pickleball court is 20 feet wide and 44 feet long for both singles and doubles. The net divides the court in half. On each side, the 7-foot area next to the net is the non-volley zone, usually called the kitchen.

The kitchen is not a place you are forbidden to enter. Its rule is narrower: you may not volley while touching the non-volley zone or its boundary line. If the ball bounces first, you may step into the kitchen and play it.

2. Decide who serves first

Pickleball can be played as doubles, with two players per side, or singles, with one. Any fair method may be used to decide who starts serving. In doubles, the player on the right-hand side begins the first service turn.

3. Serve diagonally

The serve starts every rally. It must travel diagonally into the correct service court and clear the opponent’s non-volley zone. A serve that lands on the kitchen line is short and therefore a fault.

USA Pickleball recognizes both the volley serve and the drop serve. The volley serve has specific contact requirements; the drop serve follows different motion rules. A serve may touch the net and still be legal if it clears the kitchen and lands in the correct service court.

4. Let the first two shots bounce

The receiver must let the serve bounce before returning it. Then the serving team must let that return bounce before playing the third shot. This is the two-bounce rule.

ShotWhat must happen?
ServeMust bounce on the receiving side
Return of serveMust bounce on the serving side
Third shot onwardMay be played after a bounce or as a volley, subject to the kitchen rule

The rule is often misunderstood as “two bounces on each side.” It is two required bounces total: one on each side.

5. After that, the rally is open

Once the two required bounces have occurred, either team may volley the ball or let it bounce. The rally continues until one side commits a fault: for example, hitting the ball out, failing to clear the net, allowing a second bounce, volleying illegally from the kitchen, touching the net while the ball is live, or breaking another applicable rule.

6. Understand the kitchen before you rush the net

You may stand in the kitchen, step into it, and hit a ball from it after the ball has bounced. What you cannot do is volley while touching the kitchen or its line. A volley can also become a fault if your momentum carries you into the non-volley zone afterward.

This matters because good pickleball is often played near the kitchen line. The rule prevents players from standing directly at the net and smashing balls out of the air from that position.

7. Learn the scoring system

Traditional side-out scoring remains USA Pickleball’s official baseline scoring method. A point is scored only when the serving side wins the rally.

FormatScore callServing position
SinglesServer score – receiver scoreEven server score = right; odd = left
DoublesServing score – receiving score – server numberServing order and team score determine the correct server and side

In doubles, a score such as 4–2–1 means the serving team has 4, the receiving team has 2, and server 1 is serving for that service turn. Traditional doubles begins at 0–0–2 because the first serving team starts with only one server before the first side out.

Most games are played to 11 and must be won by two, although tournament formats may use other game lengths. USA Pickleball also provisionally permits rally scoring in specified formats, so tournament players should check the event format rather than assuming every competition uses traditional side-out scoring.

8. Singles and doubles use the same court

Unlike tennis, pickleball does not shrink the playable width for singles. Singles and doubles use the same 20 × 44-foot court and the same core rally, kitchen, line and two-bounce rules.

The biggest differences are the score call, service sequence and positioning logic. Singles uses a two-number score. Doubles uses three numbers and generally gives both partners a service turn before a side out.

9. Where should a beginner stand?

USA Pickleball’s beginner positioning guidance follows directly from the two-bounce rule. The serving team should not rush forward immediately after serving because the return must bounce on its side. Starting near the baseline gives the serving team room to play that bounce.

On the receiving side, the receiver usually begins near the baseline while the non-receiving doubles partner can already be closer to the kitchen line. After the required bounces, both teams generally work toward strong positions near the non-volley-zone line rather than lingering deep in the court.

First-game positioning rule: serving team stays back for the return bounce; receiving team can establish the kitchen line earlier. That single habit prevents many beginner positioning errors.

10. What shots do you actually need?

You do not need a catalogue of advanced shots to play your first game. A beginner needs enough control to serve legally, return the serve, keep the ball in play and understand when a volley is allowed.

As the rules become automatic, the most useful next skills are a dependable return, controlled volleys, dinks, resets and a third shot that allows the serving team to move forward safely. Those are tactical skills, not additional rules.

Quick answers

How many people play pickleball?

Either two players total in singles or four players total in doubles.

Is the singles court smaller?

No. Singles and doubles use the same 20 × 44-foot court.

Can you stand in the kitchen?

Yes. You simply cannot volley while touching the non-volley zone or its line.

Do both teams have to let the ball bounce?

Yes, at the start of each rally: the receiver must let the serve bounce and the serving team must let the return bounce.

Can the serve hit the net?

Yes. If it clears the non-volley zone and lands in the correct service court, play continues.

Who scores a point?

Under traditional side-out scoring, only the serving side can score.
