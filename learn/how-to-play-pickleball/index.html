How to Play Pickleball: A Beginner’s Guide

A clear, practical introduction to pickleball rules, scoring, positioning and basic strategy.

Published August 2026 · Pickleball Cosmos

Pickleball is a paddle sport played on a compact court with a perforated plastic ball. The basic objective is simple: send the ball over the net and win the rally by forcing an error or landing a legal shot your opponent cannot return.

The basic setup

You can play singles or doubles. Doubles is the most common format. The court measures 20 feet wide by 44 feet long, and the non-volley zone — commonly called the kitchen — extends 7 feet from the net on each side.

How a point starts

The serve is hit diagonally into the opponent’s service court. Under standard rules, the ball must clear the non-volley zone. After the serve, each side must allow the ball to bounce once before volleying; this is the two-bounce rule.

How rallies develop

Once both bounces have occurred, players may volley or play the ball after a bounce. In doubles, much of the game is decided near the kitchen line, where control, patience and placement often matter more than raw power.

Beginner priority: learn the serve, return, two-bounce rule, kitchen rule and basic scoring before worrying about advanced shot selection.

What to learn next

Once you are comfortable with the basics, focus on consistent returns, moving as a pair in doubles, dinking and the third-shot drop. Those skills will take you much further than simply trying to hit harder.
