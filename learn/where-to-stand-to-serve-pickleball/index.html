Where to Stand to Serve in Pickleball

Stand behind the baseline, inside your serving lane. At the instant you strike the ball, you must be between the imaginary extensions of the centerline and sideline for the correct service court. A foot on the baseline, on the court or outside that lane is a service foot fault.

By Pickleball Cosmos EditorialReviewed August 17, 2026
Rules DeskReviewed against the 2026 USA Pickleball Official RulebookRule 7.A · Server positioning
Use this three-part test at contact: (1) at least one foot is on the ground; (2) neither foot touches the court or baseline; and (3) neither foot is outside the imaginary extensions of the sideline and centerline for the correct serving area.

1. First choose the correct side

In both singles and doubles, the serving team serves from the right-hand court when its score is even and from the left-hand court when its score is odd. At 0, 2, 4 or 6, start right. At 1, 3, 5 or 7, start left.

That rule tells you the serving lane. It does not determine where a non-serving doubles partner must stand; the partner’s positioning is a strategy choice so long as the serve itself complies with the rules.

2. Then stand behind the baseline

The baseline is part of the court. At the moment you hit the serve, neither foot may contact the playing surface or the baseline. You may begin with your body leaning forward or take a step after contact; the rule is judged at the instant of contact.

A simple self-check: place both feet clearly behind the line before beginning the motion. Do not use a toe on the paint as a reference point.

3. Stay in the lane between the two imaginary extensions

Imagine the centerline and the sideline continuing straight backward beyond the baseline. Those two invisible rails define your serving lane. On the right side, stand between the right sideline’s extension and the centerline’s extension. On the left side, stand between the left sideline’s extension and the centerline’s extension.

The rules do not require your entire body to stay inside that lane. The test is your feet at serve contact. That is why a wide follow-through is not itself a foot fault, while a foot planted outside the lane is.

4. Know the four service-position faults

At the moment the ball is struck…RulingWhy
A foot touches the baselineFaultThe baseline is part of the court.
A foot has stepped into the courtFaultThe server must be behind the baseline at contact.
A foot is beyond the sideline or centerline extensionFaultThe server is outside the correct serving area.
Both feet are airborneFaultAt least one foot must be on the ground at contact.
You step forward after contactLegalPosition is judged when the serve is hit, not after.

The position rule is separate from serve mechanics

Standing in the right place does not make every serve legal. Placement, ball release and—on a volley serve—paddle path, paddle-head position and contact height are separate tests. Conversely, a legal drop-serve motion does not excuse a foot on the baseline.

Singles versus doubles: what changes?

The court dimensions and serving-area test do not change. The difference is score administration. In singles, one player serves until a fault, then service changes. In doubles, the serving team can have a second server after losing a rally, except for the opening service sequence. The score’s first number still tells the serving team which side it uses.

Quick answers

Can you stand on the baseline when serving?

No. The baseline counts as the court for this rule. A foot touching it at contact is a service foot fault.

Can you serve from outside the sideline in pickleball?

No. At contact, neither foot may be outside the imaginary extension of the sideline and centerline that define the correct serving area.

Can you jump while serving?

Not at contact. At least one foot must be touching the ground when the serve is struck.

Does your partner have to stand behind the baseline too?

The server-position rule applies to the server. A doubles partner’s location is generally tactical, subject to the other rules of play.
